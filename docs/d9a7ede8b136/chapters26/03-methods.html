4  Samples and methods

Which samples were measured, how were water and major elements quantified, and which models turn the profiles into a rate? This chapter gives the samples (Table 4.1), the SIMS and EMPA methods (Table 4.2), the melt-inclusion treatment, and one paragraph on each model. Detail sits in the appendices: diffusion in Appendix A, melt inclusions in Appendix B, degassing in Appendix C, and data quality in Appendix D.

4.1 Localities and samples

The xenoliths and scoria come from three alkali-basalt cinder cones in the Uinkaret and Cima volcanic fields: Sullivan Ranch (SR), Vulcan’s Throne and Cima vent 16. SR is a cone south of Mt. Trumbull, sampled at two sites that probably record different magma pulses. The pit on the cone flank gave WHT06 and its scoria in place, and the roadside gave the weathered-out bombs WHT01 (also written WHT1) and GCB1. The roadside scoria holds Ol-2, the olivine whose profile sets the ascent rate. Vulcan’s Throne and Cima serve only as comparison volcanoes. All SR ascent-rate work uses SR alone. The SIMS dataset (Supplement Table S2) has 284 analyses, of which 270 are accepted.

The roadside scoria AZ09-GCB supplies the GC2 inclusions and a whole rock that is somewhat altered; the AZ09-UK01 lava is the Fe/Mg reference and the starting composition for the degassing paths. Bombs were chosen because they cool faster than xenoliths in lava; one lava-flow xenolith (CA18-Lavablock) tests the cooling rate. SR xenoliths were sectioned and polished as thick sections; Cima and Vulcan’s Throne xenoliths were crushed and clusters hand-picked, so their position in the xenolith is unknown. Samples were cleaned in acetone, toluene, distilled water and isopropanol baths, with only acetone-soluble plastics in preparation. The Sullivan Ranch material is the same cone that earlier work called Trumbull, GC2 and GCB; this report uses the name Sullivan Ranch.

4.2 Water by SIMS and NanoSIMS

Water was measured in three sessions: the Cameca 6f at the Carnegie Institution for Science in 2013 (Cima and Vulcan’s Throne) and 2018 (GCB1 and its scoria), and the Cameca NanoSIMS at Caltech in 2020 (WHT01 and WHT06, mounts A and B). The 2013 session could not measure olivine reliably above background, and the 2020 session had too high a background, so 48 of the 52 accepted olivine analyses are from 2018 and the other four are Cima olivine below the detection limit. Samples were mounted in indium, held at 90 °C under vacuum for 2 days, and kept under ultrahigh vacuum (10^-9 Torr) for 72 hours before analysis. A Cs+ beam measured 16O1H, 12C, 19F, 31P, 32S and 35Cl relative to 30Si.

Calibration. Each session has one line per phase from standards run in the same session: H2O (ppm) = slope × (16O1H/30Si × SiO2 wt%) + intercept, fitted by orthogonal-distance regression with bootstrapped uncertainties. The opx line uses the revised KBH-1 value of 202 ppm (±15; 217 before), which scales earlier opx concentrations by 0.93. Olivine is reduced on the opx line in all sessions. That puts the olivine data within about 12 % of the Withers (2012) olivine line, and the olivine/melt partition coefficient K = 9 ppm per wt% (Towbin et al. 2023) was built on the same scale, so data and K share one calibration. Placing olivine exactly on the Withers line would be equivalent to K of about 10.1 and would lower the isothermal Ol-2 rate from 0.155 to about 0.125 MPa/s. One NanoSIMS opx (T1-216, in Cima) was re-reduced on the opx line, giving 129.70 ppm against 131.26 ppm as deposited.

Uncertainty. Each analysis carries a total 1 sigma, the square root of the summed squares of the calibration term (the larger side of the 5–95 % calibration interval divided by 1.645), the counting error and the blank scatter beyond counting. It replaces the deposited combined uncertainty, which understated the calibration term. The median total 1 sigma is 0.34 ppm for olivine (SR olivine at most 0.59 ppm; the four Cima olivine about 2.9 ppm), 11 ppm for opx and 22 ppm for cpx. The four Ol-2 spots carry 0.31–0.43 ppm.

Detection limits. For each line, L_C (decision limit) and L_D (detection limit) follow Currie (1968) from the scatter of Suprasil blanks beyond counting statistics, and LoQ is the concentration at 20 % relative uncertainty (Table 4.2). Six accepted values lie below L_D (four Cima olivine and two Cima cpx from CA18-05x) and two lie between L_D and LoQ (SR scoria cpx xenocrysts). Every SR olivine value (2.2–8.7 ppm) is above the 2018 LoQ of 1.44 ppm.

4.3 Electron microprobe

Major elements were measured on the Cameca SX100 at the American Museum of Natural History in 2018 (GCB1) and on the Cameca Tactis over several sessions from 2020 to early 2022. EMPA supplies the SiO2 used in the SIMS reduction, the Mg# and tetrahedral Al of the pyroxenes (used in the cpx partitioning test), and the olivine Fo profiles. Phases were assigned by composition (cpx CaO of at least 15 wt%; opx CaO below 3 wt% and MgO 28–37 wt%). Mg# is molar with all Fe as FeO. No usable EMPA exists for several Cima and Vulcan’s Throne grains and for the WHT06_2 xenolith, so those cpx have no tetrahedral-Al value.

4.4 Melt inclusions

Inclusions are naturally glassy and olivine-hosted, hand-picked from the SR scoria (AZ18-06 from the pit and AZ09-GCB from the roadside), the Cima scoria MO10-16 and the Vulcan’s Throne scoria. Glass H2O and CO2 were measured by SIMS, and major elements by EMPA. Seven SR inclusions also have LA-ICP-MS trace elements [CHECK: LA-ICP-MS instrument and protocol are not in the sources read]. Twelve SR inclusions are used; GC2_ol4 has no measured volatiles, and GC2_ol6 is excluded because its glass is too magnesian for its host, which points to olivine in the analysis.

Overlap correction. For AZ09-GCB and Vulcan’s Throne, the SIMS volatiles are the Plank-lab values corrected for beam overlap with the host olivine (factors 1.14–1.67, with Cl corrected further by 1.239). This is not the correction for post-entrapment crystallization. The formula is the Plank lab’s [CHECK: formula and its source]. The AZ18 inclusions carry the revised SIMS H2O and CO2 and new EMPA analyses; Cima values are uncorrected.

MiMiC. MiMiC (Rasmussen et al. 2020) adds or removes host olivine until each inclusion is in Fe–Mg equilibrium with its host (Kd from Toplis 2005; Fe3+/FeT fixed at 0.2 for SR), then adds back the CO2 that the vapor bubble holds with a bubble-growth model. The published code takes the bubble CO2 volume from a Redlich–Kwong pure-CO2 fluid scaled by the CO2 mole fraction; two errors in it partly cancel. The production run replaces this volume with the Duan and Zhang (2006) H2O–CO2 mixture at MiMiC’s own vapor composition. For the SR inclusions the change adds 0 to 15 % to bubble CO2 and raises the saturation pressure with bubble CO2 (Pvbg) by 0 to 7.6 %, and it leaves every inclusion H2O unchanged. The inclusion tables use the production run. The P–T path keeps the base (published) MiMiC, because the two give the same path: a slope through the inclusions of 98.5 °C/GPa (68 % range 89.1–108.5) with the base run and 98.3 with the Duan and Zhang run, and entrapment temperatures differ by at most 1.2 °C. Other options, including a Fe-loss correction and other solubility models, are in Appendix B.

4.5 Models

Degassing path. The path of melt H2O against pressure is a closed-system MagmaSat path at 1200 °C on the AZ09-UK01 lava composition (Fe3+/FeT 0.167). The primary start is the published MiMiC melt, 3.549 wt% H2O with 8,529 ppm CO2, which saturates at 682 MPa (Chapter 6). On this path the melt loses half its H2O only below about 55 MPa. The second scenario, SR-1 (the CO2/Nb start), is 3.59 wt% H2O with 14,100 ppm CO2; it saturates at 922 MPa and loses half its H2O below 63 MPa. Iacono-Marziano is the alternative solubility model and gives a lower saturation pressure (Appendix C).

P–T path. Temperature falls along a straight line from 1330 °C at 2.26 GPa (Plank and Forsyth 2016) to the surface, with a slope drawn from U(46, 120) °C/GPa. This is equivalent to an isothermal fit at about 1150 °C, the mean SR entrapment temperature. The isothermal 1200 °C fit is kept as the simple case.

Diffusion fits. Pressure falls linearly in a one-dimensional slab whose rim holds the equilibrium H2O of the degassing melt (Appendix A). The Bayesian fit returns a posterior median and 68 % range for the rate. Olivine uses the Ferriss et al. (2018) [100] law, D = 10^-5.4 exp(−130 kJ/RT) m²/s, with K = 9 ppm per wt%. Pyroxene K is anchored on opx: Novella et al. (2014) multiplied by 0.93 gives 117.7 ppm per wt%, and cpx K follows the measured cpx/opx core ratio. A temperature that falls along the path enters through a warped pressure, the integral of D(T)/D_ref over pressure, so the constant-D solver gives the variable-temperature result exactly and reproduces the isothermal fits when the ratio is 1.

Joint fit. One continuous ascent is fitted to Ol-2 and to the WHT1 exterior opx and cpx together, with the pyroxene diffusivities free. Stalls are tested against it.

Xenolith exchange. A nested model lets each WHT1 pyroxene exchange with a grain-boundary network of diffusivity D_gb whose water activity follows the melt at the xenolith exterior, with a boundary-drop factor g for each position. It is fitted to all five WHT1 traverses, with the D_gb activation energy set to 34 kJ/mol (Demouchy 2010).
