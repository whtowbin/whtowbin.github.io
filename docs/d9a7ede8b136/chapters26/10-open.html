11  Open questions for co-authors

Which choices could still change a finding, and which of them need an expert call rather than more analysis? The list below is short on purpose: it names only the items that could move a conclusion.

11.1 Questions for Terry

CO2/Nb as the second scenario for the starting melt. The primary start is the published MiMiC melt (3.549 wt% H2O, 8,529 ppm CO2, saturation 682 MPa). The inclusions lost CO2 before trapping, so that path starts partly degassed: bubble-reconstructed CO2 reaches only 9,079 ppm, and the inclusions saturate at 129–574 MPa, far shallower than xenolith pickup (1.15 GPa). The second scenario adds CO2 back as CO2/Nb times the Nb of the lava (59 ppm). With the depleted-mantle ratio of 239 (Saal et al. 2002) the start has 14,100 ppm CO2 and saturates at 922 MPa, close to pickup. With the ocean-island ratio of 505 ± 168 (Rosenthal et al. 2015) it has 29,800 ppm and saturates at 1,438 MPa, deeper than pickup. The rate depends on the choice by about 10 % (joint fit 0.100 MPa/s for the MiMiC start against 0.112 for CO2/Nb; Ol-2 isothermal 0.143 against 0.155), but the depth story does not survive without it. Terry is asked to judge whether the CO2/Nb scenario should stay as a second scenario or be dropped.

  • Which ratio? Is 239 or 505 the better reference for an enriched alkaline basalt?
  • Which Nb? Should each inclusion use its own LA-ICP-MS Nb instead of the lava value? With each inclusion’s Nb and a ratio of 239, undegassed CO2 is 11,400–20,500 ppm and saturation 670–900 MPa (Iacono-Marziano), against 190–450 MPa as measured.
  • Whether to correct at all. Is the correction worth making for a summary of this kind? CO2/Ba gave 3–7× more CO2 and was rejected, because the SR Ba/Nb (11.5–16.5) lies far above that of the reference pairs.

11.2 Model questions

Cpx partitioning. The cores hold 1.2–2.6× the water that the recalibrated experiments predict (Table 9.2). Is the cause pre-entrainment water, a wetter melt than the inclusions record, a higher D at these conditions, or the recalibration of the experiments? No test separates them yet.

Opx starting levels. The opx plateaus at 300–320 ppm and the deepest points at 409–413 ppm can be read as K uncertainty (K of 84–89 against the anchored 117.7 ppm per wt%) or as different starting H2O (the mixture and the wetter batch). The rate model fits with the MiMiC (or CO2/Nb) start and the exchange model prefers the lower one (Section 9.2). Is there independent evidence to choose a K?

Transport laws on the P–T path. The two tables on transport mechanisms (mechanism curves and transport estimates) still run on the earlier mixture path. They need temperature laws for melt and vapor transport before they can be moved to the P–T path, and the grain-boundary activation energy (34 kJ/mol) is taken from one experimental study. Should they then use the single median slope (82.8 °C/GPa) or the full slope family?

Path shape. The olivine profile fixes the rate over the last ~60 MPa but not the deep rate (Section 8.5). - Constraint: a magma that accelerated 2 to 5 times toward the surface would have a mean ascent rate from pickup 2 to 5 times below the headline. - Test: only a phase that loses water deeper could tell, such as the cpx rims, which form deeper than the opx and olivine rims, or an inclusion record of the CO2 loss. Is any such test worth making?

11.3 Data questions still open

  • Matched exterior pyroxenes. The modeled exterior opx (0.5 mm) and cpx (7.5 mm) come from different exteriors of WHT1, so their rates are not a like-for-like pair (Section 8.3). Should the pyroxene fits be repeated with a matched pair: Exterior-2 opx-1 and cpx-1 (both 7.5 mm), or Exterior-1 opx-2 and cpx-2 (0.5 and 1.45 mm)?

  • Cpx calibration standard. A NanoSIMS standard row named “opx_ignore” sits in the cpx fit. If it is not intended, every cpx concentration changes slightly, and so does the partitioning test.

  • Experimental scale factor. The thesis workbook’s “Kd-corr” is exactly 0.75 times the published-scale D. Does that come from the SIMS-scale factor of the cpx experiments (mean 0.769)?

  • Roadside final melt. No inclusion or matrix glass samples the melt that set the roadside rims, so the starting H of Ol-2 is unmeasured. A drier carrier would make the rate faster (the 2.72 wt% start gives 1.12×).

  • Rounded cpx line and scaling. Fifteen cpx analyses were typed with a rounded calibration line (0.2–1.4 ppm low), and two (T1-218 and T1-219) were rescaled for SiO2. Small, but should they stay as in the SIMS dataset (Supplement Table S2)?
