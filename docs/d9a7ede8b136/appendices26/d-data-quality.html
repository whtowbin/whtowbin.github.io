Appendix D — Data quality

This appendix documents how far the SIMS dataset can be trusted: the calibration lines, what uncertainty is reported and why, the detection limits, the one re-reduced analysis, the electron-microprobe coverage, the disagreements between the deposited text and the data, the checks on the compiled workbook, and the olivine Fo populations. The measurements and their use are in Chapter 4 and Chapter 5.

D.1 Calibration

The calibration question is whether the olivine data and the olivine/melt K that converts them to melt H2O are on the same scale. Scaling the olivine data by a factor s has the same effect on a fitted rate as using K/s, so a mismatch between them would bias the rate directly.

Each analysis is reduced as x = 16O1H/30Si × SiO2 (wt%) of the analysis, with no further division, and H2O (ppm) = slope × x + intercept. Each line is an orthogonal-distance regression of the session’s standards plus its blank rows, with the x error from the ratio scatter and the y error the accepted standard error, followed by a 5000-draw bootstrap.

  • The SIMS dataset uses the revised opx standards. Towbin et al. (2023) revised KBH-1 from 217 ± 11 to 202 ± 15 ppm, based on the Bell et al. (1995) manometry (186 ppm measured, plus a 14 % upward correction that the paper treats as a maximum). ROM-273-OG2 (263 to 245) and India enstatite (141 to 131) were revised with it, so earlier opx concentrations scale by 0.93.
  • Olivine is reduced on the opx line in all sessions. With the revision, the opx SIMS line is 12 % above the Withers (2012) olivine line, against 19 % before. The 2023 paper treats this as within uncertainty.
  • The olivine/melt K is on the same scale. Towbin et al. (2023) built K by recalibrating the published experiments (Koga 2003, Aubaud 2004, Hauri 2006; Newcombe 2020 inclusion pairs) onto this calibration. The revised mean partition coefficient is 0.0009, the K = 9 ppm per wt% used for the olivine rate.
  • The remaining calibration uncertainty is the 12 %. Placing the olivine exactly on the Withers (2012) line is equivalent to K ≈ 10.1, which gives about 0.125 MPa/s against 0.155 MPa/s for the isothermal wet-start fit.
  • Two older scales are superseded, not alternatives. The old Bell calibration (× 1.352 of the SIMS dataset) and the “Withers” column of the 2018 raw workbook (× 0.789) are not used. The workbook column does not match the Withers (2012) line, which would be about 0.89 times the revised opx values; it is probably an older implementation without the blank correction the paper applies. Sensitivity runs on the × 0.789 column (an earlier stall fit) are on a superseded scale.
  • The pyroxene diffusivities carry their own scale factors (opx × 0.93; a separate cpx factor), treated in Appendix A.

The per-session standards (glass, olivine, opx and cpx reference materials, plus Suprasil and synthetic forsterite as blanks) are listed in the calibration table of the supplement; the text of the deposited chapter lists 2013-only standards among the 2018 ones [CHECK: per-session standard lists, supplement Table S6 block D]. The NanoSIMS glass line is printed as 143 on the calibration figure against 147.6 recomputed (an old label); the glass line is not used for minerals, so no action was taken.

D.2 Total 1σ against the deposited combined uncertainty

The deposited “combined uncertainty” is \(\sqrt{((P_{95} - \mathrm{best})^2 + (\mathrm{best} - P_5)^2)/16 + s_{SIMS}^2}\). It reproduces every deposited value but it understates the calibration term: it carries about 0.58 of the calibration 1σ. The SIMS dataset therefore reports the total 1σ

\[ \sigma_{tot} = \sqrt{\sigma_{cal}^2 + \sigma_{count}^2 + \sigma_0^2}, \tag{D.1}\]

where \(\sigma_{cal}\) is the larger side of the 5–95 % calibration interval divided by 1.645, \(\sigma_{count}\) the counting standard error and \(\sigma_0\) the Suprasil blank scatter beyond counting statistics, times the slope. Examples: T1-181 (2.20 ppm) goes from 0.095 to 0.30 ppm, and T1-056 (206 ppm) from 10.5 to 18.8 ppm. The Ol-2 spots carry 0.31–0.43 ppm, and 2013 analyses, which had no uncertainty, now have one. The compiled data also keep the deposited column, renamed “as deposited”. The ascent fits do not read these uncertainties: each fit uses a fixed σ per profile (Section A.3), so the change affects only the reported values. The 2023 edition of the supplement keeps the deposited values.

The calibration term is shared by all points of a session and line, so it acts like a scale (K) error rather than scatter; moving it into the K prior changes nothing visible in the rates (Section A.3).

D.3 Detection limits

Three limits (Table D.2) are quoted per session and line, computed from the Suprasil blank scatter in the same unit as the samples (x above), with blanks entering at their own SiO2. Suprasil is the near-blank (nominally 1 ± 1 ppm, likely 1–2 ppm); the NanoSIMS synthetic forsterite blank is probably SynFo70, which carries real H, and is not used.

Each analysis is tagged against L_D and LoQ and every measured value is kept (no substitution). The limits are in Table D.1. Of the 284 analyses, six are below L_D: T1-039 and T1-040 (NanoSIMS cpx, CA18-05) and T1-158 to T1-161 (2013 Cima olivine); T1-135 and T1-136 (NanoSIMS pit cpx) lie between L_D and LoQ; 276 are quantified, including every 2018 olivine. For the 2018 olivine, L_D is 0.99 ppm and LoQ 1.44 ppm. Because the Cima olivine values are below L_D, the SR-to-Cima olivine ratio is not quoted.

The other volatiles are less certain. Blanks from the 6f sessions show that F in pyroxene and P are real signals, while S, Cl and CO2 in these minerals sit at or near the blank level, so their tabulated values are counting statistics only and carry no calibration or blank term. The supplement keeps H2O, F and P as the main volatiles and gives S, Cl and CO2 in one note.

D.4 T1-216 on the opx line

T1-216 (analysis CA18-05x-OPX_Core) is an opx analysis that was reduced at SiO2 55 and, as deposited, not on the opx line. The 2026 edition and the compiled data use its opx-line value, 129.70 ppm (90 % interval 119.93–139.09), from the opx columns of the 2023 processing sheet, against 131.26 ppm as deposited. It is the maximum of the Cima xenolith opx (35–130 ppm in the 2026 edition). The 2023 edition and the figure data keep 131.26.

D.5 Electron microprobe coverage

EMPA supplies the SiO2 used in the SIMS reduction, the Mg# and Cr of the pyroxenes, and the olivine Fo profiles. Coverage is uneven.

  • SR. The compiled EMPA table has one analysis per SR xenolith: cpx Mg# 91.7 (GCB1), 90.9 (WHT1) and 92.5 (WHT06_1). Per-grain 2018 data for GCB1 were recovered (82 points; 9 cpx and 7 opx grains linked to SIMS grains) and added as a separate table. The GCB1 grains are uniform: cpx Mg# 91.3–91.8, opx 90.3–90.4.
  • One phase label corrected. The GCB1 spot R-21_OPX-1 B, named as opx in the earlier files, is cpx by EMPA (renamed R-21_CPX-1); the SIMS dataset already lists it as cpx and its value is unchanged.
  • No usable EMPA exists for VT8504, VT8505, VT8506, VT8536, VT8535 cluster 3, cip98-19 clusters 1, 8 and 10, CA18-05x, CA18-Lavablock, or the WHT06_2 xenolith grains, after a search of every EMPA session found.
  • Olivine Fo profiles come from the EMPA master sheet of the melt-vein study (phenocrysts, xenocrysts, WHT1 exterior grains and melt-pocket overgrowths).

D.7 Compiled-workbook checks

scripts/build_compiled_workbook.py builds the compiled workbook from the final chapter tables and the raw session files, scripts/recalc.py recalculates it and scripts/verify_compiled.py checks it. The SIMS dataset is copied verbatim from the 2023 chapter tables; cleaned columns (phase, locality, position, core/rim, flag, grain ID) are added beside the original ones, and the 2018 GCB1 EMPA is a separate table. The last full check gave:

  • 284 rows with 0 cell differences from the source table;
  • every row linked to a raw instrument row with the identical H2O value (64 rows from the 2013 6f session, 103 from 2018, 117 from NanoSIMS 2020); all 117 NanoSIMS rows also linked to a ratio row with no mismatch;
  • 0 lookup mismatches in the data behind the regenerated chapter figures;
  • 82 GCB1 EMPA points with 0 mismatches against the raw export, and 14 of 15 grains linked to SIMS grains.

D.8 Olivine Fo populations

The olivine Fo data separate the crystals by origin (Table D.4) (scripts/olivine_fo_populations.py, data/olivine_fo_populations/grains.csv).

  • Ol-2 grew early, from a more primitive melt. Its core plateau sits about 3 Fo units above lava equilibrium and above every WHT06 phenocryst core except ol40 (88.1–88.2). It was then re-equilibrated toward the carrier from the rim. A late, shallow origin is unlikely, which supports starting the H2O model in equilibrium with the deep melt.
  • The two localities had different final histories. The roadside rims need a melt with MgO 6.6–8.6 wt% (MgO/FeOT 0.58–0.74), about 6–11 % olivine removed from the AZ09-GCB whole rock (Section B.12).
  • Fe–Mg diffusion times (1250 °C, 500 MPa) are 0.15–0.37 d for the roadside xenocrysts, 1.9–3.0 d for the pit xenocrysts and about 18 d (15–22) for Ol-2 along [100] (Section A.10).
  • Inclusion H2O has no usable Fo trend. The SR correlation (r = 0.54) is carried by the two Ol36 inclusions in Fo 79 hosts; among the Fo 84–86 hosts H2O follows Cl, not Fo.

D.9 Measurement methods

SIMS and NanoSIMS. The SR xenolith sections, scoria phenocrysts and xenocrysts were analyzed on the Cameca 6f SIMS at the Carnegie Institution for Science in 2018 (GCB1 and its scoria) and on the Cameca NanoSIMS at Caltech in 2020 (WHT01 and WHT06, Mounts A and B). The Cima and Vulcan’s Throne samples were analyzed on the Carnegie 6f in 2013, following Lloyd et al. (2013) and Newcombe et al. (2020). The 2013 session could not measure olivine above background routinely, and the 2020 session had too high a background for olivine, so 48 of the 52 olivine analyses are from 2018. Samples were cleaned in acetone, toluene, distilled water and isopropanol baths, with only acetone-soluble plastics used in preparation, mounted in indium, held at 90 °C under vacuum for 2 days and held under ultrahigh vacuum (10^-9 Torr) for 72 hours before analysis. A Cs+ beam measured 16O1H, 12C, 19F, 31P, 32S and 35Cl relative to 30Si. Calibration curves were built for each phase from standards analyzed in the same session. Other protocols followed Towbin et al. (2023), including the revised opx standard values.

EMPA. Major elements were measured on the Cameca SX100 at the American Museum of Natural History in 2018 (GCB1) and on the Cameca Tactis in several sessions from summer 2020 to February 2022 (protocols as in Chapter 2). The GCB1 per-grain data were recovered from the July 2018 workbook and checked point by point against the raw instrument export. Phases were assigned by composition (cpx CaO ≥ 15 wt%; opx CaO < 3 and MgO 28–37 wt%); Mg# is molar with all Fe as FeO.

Olivine Fo populations. The script reads the “Olivine” sheet of the melt-vein EMPA master workbook with bad and ignored points removed, assigns each profile to a population (phenocryst, xenocryst in scoria, WHT1 exterior grain, melt-pocket overgrowth, xenolith olivine), takes the core Fo as the median beyond 70 % of the profile length and the rim Fo as the analysis nearest the edge. Olivine in equilibrium with the AZ09-UK01 lava was computed for Fe3+/FeT 0.167–0.20 and Kd 0.30–0.34 (Fo 83.6–85.7).
